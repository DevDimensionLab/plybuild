## Ordinary Behavior And Qualification

The real clock delegates directly to `time`. Fake clocks serialize clock and
waiter state under a mutex, use one-slot nonblocking timer/ticker channels,
sort expirations, run `AfterFunc` callbacks in caller-independent goroutines,
and expose explicit blocking helpers for deterministic tests. Callers own
their callbacks, contexts, clock advancement, and Stop/Cancel lifecycle. The
ordinary positive fixture covers timers, AfterFunc, buffered ticker drops, 64
concurrent waiters, blocking, advancing, and stopping; it passes race
count-ten for v0.4.0/v0.5.0 under exact Go and for v0.4.0 under Go 1.18.10.

No release passes the full contract:

- Selected v0.1.0 treats only exactly zero as immediate. Its fake
  `After(-time.Second)` registers a sleeper instead of firing immediately as
  `time.After` does. Its `BlockUntil` notification also matches only an exact
  waiter count, so a caller can remain blocked if registrations skip past the
  requested count.
- V0.2.0-v0.3.0 add fake tickers but do not reject zero or negative periods as
  `time.NewTicker` does. Their ticker loops can spin or reach invalid period
  arithmetic. V0.3.0 adds timer validation but leaves fake ticker construction
  unchanged.
- V0.4.0 is the highest Go-1.18-compatible release. `Ticker.Reset` appends the
  same ticker without removing its old schedule and does not update its stored
  period. The focused fixture proves that it fires at the old interval after
  reset and that Reset-then-Stop leaves a scheduled duplicate which fires.
  Fake `NewTicker` and `Ticker.Reset` also accept zero and negative durations
  instead of panicking. All four contract failures reproduce under exact Go
  1.26.7 and Go 1.18.10.
- V0.5.0 validates `NewTicker` and updates the stored reset period, but Reset
  still appends a duplicate schedule, Reset-then-Stop still fires, and Reset
  still accepts non-positive durations. Its fake context `Err` blocks before
  `Done` instead of returning nil, calling Cancel leaves the deadline timer in
  the fake-clock waiter set, and the implementation cancels on a parent's
  `DeadlineExceeded` despite its own documentation saying that condition is
  ignored. These five deterministic contract failures are independent of its
  Go 1.21 floor and incompatible exported API.

Timers with non-positive durations otherwise fire immediately, tick delivery
is intentionally nonblocking and coalescing, stopped/reset timer return values
are mutex-protected, and deterministic callers can use `NewFakeClockAt`.
`NewFakeClock` deliberately samples system time. BlockUntil without a context
can deadlock when the caller's expected count is wrong; the cancellable form
bounds caller blocking but leaves a cancelled blocker registered until a
future waiter count satisfies it. These ownership and lifecycle boundaries do
not cure the reproduced failures.

Therefore v0.4.0 is the highest floor-compatible candidate, but is not
qualified. V0.5.0 is neither floor-compatible nor behavior/API qualified.
Selected v0.1.0 is stable and floor-compatible but is not behavior qualified.
